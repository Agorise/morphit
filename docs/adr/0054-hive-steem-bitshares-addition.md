# ADR-0054: Hive (HIVE), Steem (STEEM) and BitShares (BTS) Addition — Trade-Only Graphene Coins Paid to Account Names

**Status:** Accepted

**Context:**

Hive, Steem and BitShares are delegated-proof-of-stake chains of the Graphene family Blurt belongs to: elected witnesses produce a block every 3 seconds, and a transfer goes from one named account to another, with an optional memo. BitShares moved to Graphene in 2015 and has a built-in exchange where anyone can issue tokens; Steem launched in 2016 on code derived from it; Hive split off from Steem on 20 March 2020, and Blurt from Steem too. Facts below were checked in each chain's code or on the sites named, on 2026-11-09:

- **Account names.** Steem and Hive: `STEEM_MIN/MAX_ACCOUNT_NAME_LENGTH` and `HIVE_MIN/MAX_ACCOUNT_NAME_LENGTH` are 3 and 16 (libraries/protocol config.hpp of steemit/steem and openhive-network/hive), the rule Blurt kept. BitShares: `is_valid_name` (bitshares-core libraries/protocol/account.cpp) takes 1 to 63 characters (`GRAPHENE_MIN/MAX_ACCOUNT_NAME_LENGTH`), dot-separated labels, each starting with a letter, ending with a letter or digit, letters, digits and dashes between.
- **Units.** HIVE and STEEM: 3 decimals (`STEEM_BLOCKCHAIN_PRECISION_DIGITS 3`; Hive kept Steem's asset format). BTS: 5 decimals (`GRAPHENE_BLOCKCHAIN_PRECISION_DIGITS 5`).
- **Transaction ids.** 40 hex characters (RIPEMD-160) on all three, the format Blurt has too. The same string can name a transaction on each chain.
- **Fees.** Hive and Steem charge no transfer fee (resource credits). BitShares charges a small fee in BTS per transfer.
- **Memos.** Exchanges credit deposits to one shared account by the memo. Hive and Steem memos are public unless encrypted (they then start with `#`); BitShares wallets encrypt the memo text. In every case who paid whom, how much and when are public.
- **Freezing.** No issuer can freeze HIVE, STEEM or BTS. A BitShares asset issuer can freeze its own token, never BTS. By fork, though: Hive's launch gave no HIVE to the accounts that had backed the takeover of Steem (coinbureau.com's Hive review), and Steem's hard fork 0.23 in May 2020 took about 23.6 million STEEM from 64 accounts (CoinDesk). The privacy guides say so; no warning chip, since no one holds a standing power to freeze.
- **Explorers.** Hive: hivescan.info, the HAF block explorer the Hive core developers run (official block_explorer_ui). Its transaction page is `/tx/{txid}`: the owner's survey of Hive markets and explorers (9 Nov 2026) found `/transaction/{txid}` (the path named in gitlab.syncad.com/hive/block_explorer_ui issue 526) answering with an error and `/tx/` serving a page titled "Transaction 666ece4145df…" for a 2024 transaction, which we saw too. Then hivehub.dev and hive.ausbit.dev (`/tx/{txid}`, both confirmed by that survey) and ecosynthesizer.com (`/hive/transaction/{txid}`, whose server-rendered page showed a real transaction at block 105,615,595). hiveblocks.com is frozen (October 2024). Steem: steemscan.com (`/transaction/{txid}`, a client-routed page the owner's survey of Steem markets and explorers saw showing a transaction), then ecosynthesizer.com (`/steem/trx/{txid}`, server-rendered, showed a 2016 transaction at block 4,831,673; its data comes from the SteemWorld API, whose last irreversible block on 9 Nov 2026 was block 110,590,041 of 20 October, so a fresh payment may be missing there) and steemblockexplorer.com (`/tx/{txid}`, whose page loads but was not seen showing data). steemd.com is dead; the 2016 Steem onions are retired v2 addresses. BitShares: www.bitshareschain.com (`/tx/{txid}` answers as a transaction lookup; `/txid/` is a 404). The owner's own survey of BitShares markets and explorers (9 Nov 2026) found it the one explorer serving fresh data to a non-browser client (block 115,974,515), the others (blocksights.info, btslens.pages.dev, bitshares.network, the wallets' built-in explorers) browser apps, and bts.ai, cryptofresh.com, bitsharescan.com and open-explorer.io dead or stale; no second BitShares explorer that opens a transaction by its id was found. The same survey found no official `.onion` or `.i2p` address for any BitShares exchange, front-end or explorer, and the Hive survey none for any Hive explorer, API node, front-end or exchange; none is known for Steem either. It also notes a look-alike "BitShares (BTS)" BEP-20 token on BNB Smart Chain: real BTS is only the BitShares chain's core asset (id 1.3.0), and a `0x…` address fails the BTS account-name check.
- **Looking a payment up.** Hive: HAfAH, the account-history service in the Hive core developers' repository (gitlab.syncad.com/hive/HAfAH), served as plain GET JSON by many API nodes: `/hafah-api/transactions/<id>` gives the transaction, its `block_num` and the block's `timestamp` (an unknown id is an HTTP 400 whose message says "Unknown Transaction"), `/hafah-api/headblock` the newest block it holds (reversible ones included), `/hafah-api/sync-status` its last irreversible block. Seen answering on 9 Nov 2026 at api.hive.blog, api.openhive.network, techcoderx.com, rpc.mahdiyari.info and hiveapi.actifit.io. Amounts come in Hive's NAI form (`{"amount": "28941000", "precision": 3, "nai": "@@000000021"}` for HIVE; HBD is `@@000000013`). Steem: JSON-RPC (POST) only; `condenser_api.get_transaction` (steemit/steem account_history_api: "Unknown Transaction <id>" for an unknown id, a node without the transaction index says so differently), `condenser_api.get_dynamic_global_properties` (`head_block_number`, `last_irreversible_block_num`) and `condenser_api.get_block_header` (`timestamp`); amounts as "2.500 STEEM". api.steemit.com, api.justyy.com and api.steememory.com answered their health check on 9 Nov 2026; a POST could not be sent from where this was written, so the Steem calls rest on the code and the API documentation (developers.steem.io). SteemWorld's SDS, the one GET service, was 20 days behind (above). BitShares: no transaction lookup by id is served by its nodes (the owner's survey).
- **Prices.** CoinGecko's API ids `hive`, `steem`, `bitshares`; CryptoCompare's symbols `HIVE`, `STEEM`, `BTS` (each site's coin page). The onion pricenodes are not known to quote them.

## Decision

### 1. Trade-only, single-network, transparent

Each coin: `canPayListingFee: false` (the `fee_method` enum stays frozen at `blurt | btc | xmr | waived_first_buy`), `supportedNetworks: ['mainnet']`, `COIN_PRIVACY_CLASS = 'transparent'`, `optInPrivacyTech: null`, `freshAddressAdvice: 'account-reuse'` (the address is the account), no privacy-warning chip.

### 2. The address is the account name; the coin is named in words

HIVE and STEEM take their chains' `is_valid_account_name` (steemit/steem libraries/protocol/authority.cpp, which Hive kept): 3 to 16 characters, dot-separated parts of at least 3, each starting with a letter and ending with a letter or digit — every such name is a valid Blurt name too. BTS takes bitshares-core's rule. Because the same name can be an account on several of these chains (and the BTS rule also accepts any all-lowercase bech32 or CashAddr string, a valid BitShares name), `address-shape-overlap-smoke` documents the overlaps, and the chat address card of each carries a standing warning (`chat.address.account_coin_warning`): send the coin on its own chain, to the receiver's own account, with the trade's memo; an exchange deposit account needs the exchange's own memo instead; the same name can belong to someone else on another chain.

### 3. The trade's own memo; no payment link

The account is the same in every trade, and anyone can read every transfer into it. A buyer could otherwise cite the transaction id of someone else's payment into the seller's account (another buyer's, an exchange withdrawal) as his own, and nothing in it would say it was not this trade's (the web review of this release found this before it shipped). So, as for BLURT, the address card of these coins carries a short random memo (`generateBlurtMemo`: 8 lowercase letters and digits, never starting with `#`, so a Hive wallet does not encrypt it): the share window adds it, and requires it where a crypto-for-crypto seller is paid; the buyer puts it on the transfer; the HIVE / STEEM check counts only transfers carrying it (decision 7), and a BTS seller sees it in his wallet, which decrypts the memo sent to him. The memo says nothing about the trade to anyone reading the chain. An exchange deposit account still cannot be used: the exchange credits a deposit only by its own memo, where the trade's goes; every text that explains the coin says to share one's own account. No payment URI is built: wallets agree on none (Hive's `hive://sign/` links carry a whole signing request), so `buildPaymentUri` returns the bare account name, as for BLURT.

### 4. Each chain's own jitter

HIVE and STEEM add 0 to 0.099 (3 decimals, as BLURT); BTS adds 0 to 0.00999 (5 decimals). Round up only.

### 5. Explorer links only to that chain's explorers

The bundled lists above; `MORPHIT_FRONTEND_{HIVE,STEEM,BTS}_CHAT_LINK_URL` overrides each. Pages opened over Tor or I2P show no link for these coins (no hidden explorer known), unless the operator sets a `.onion` / `.i2p` one.

### 6. Display prices from CoinGecko and CryptoCompare

`COIN_QUOTE_IDS` carries the three; they are left out of `PRICENODE_COINS`. Display only, as for every coin but BLURT, BTC and XMR.

### 7. HIVE and STEEM payments checked in the chat; BTS in the wallet

A HIVE or STEEM payment recorded in a trade chat is checked live, as a BTC or XMR one is (`/v1/payment-check`, `fee/grapheneTxCheck.ts`): by its transaction id and the ask's memo (`memo=`), on the nodes above (`MORPHIT_INDEXER_HIVE_PAYMENT_NODES`, `MORPHIT_INDEXER_STEEM_PAYMENT_NODES`), two operators agreeing on the block, the amount and the memo verdict; only `transfer` operations to the account in the coin itself, carrying the memo, count, and one without it is reported (`memo_mismatch`, "not this trade's payment"). An ask without a memo binds nothing, so a payment seen there is the seller's own call. A payment in a block older than the account share is refused too, as for a reused BTC address. A node that cannot be reached, is too slow (its answer read in full within the timeout) or answers 429 / 5xx is cooled by the pool; two "not found" end the round, and HAfAH is asked for the head and the irreversible block only when it has the transaction. Depth reaches 3 only once the block is irreversible, and until then the payment shows arriving but releases nothing, as for BLURT. The 3-second blocks make the poll faster: every 6 s in the chat, a confirming answer reused 5 s by the indexer. BTS: no lookup by id, so the seller checks his wallet (`chat.address.reuse_account_body`). The nodes are clearnet only: a Tor/I2P-only indexer answers `unavailable`, and the seller checks his wallet there too.

## Consequences

- Orders in HIVE, STEEM and BTS are refused (`asset_invalid`) by sites still on v1.23.5 or older and replayed at their first start on v1.24.0 (`reapplyObsoleteRejections`), as for any added coin (`docs/ADDING-A-COIN.md`, step 10): publish, upgrade the sites, then announce the coins.
- The coin icons are the three projects' logos the owner supplied, cropped to a square with the margin the other coin icons have and minified (BitShares' came as a picture and was traced to a vector).
- A HIVE or STEEM payment is checked by the seller's indexer on those chains' public API nodes (decision 7), so the indexer reaches more outside hosts; a BTS payment is confirmed by the seller in their own wallet, as for every coin without a live check.
